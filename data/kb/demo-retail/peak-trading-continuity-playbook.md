# Peak trading continuity playbook

> Synthetic demo document. "Demo Retail" and every company, person, code and figure in this
> document are fictional and exist only to demonstrate Keelson. Not real guidance.

## 1. Purpose and scope

This playbook tells the merchandise planning, supply and logistics teams of Demo Retail how to keep
stores and the online shop in stock during **peak trading**: ISO weeks 44 to 52, with Black Friday
week (W48) as the most important single week of the year. It covers outerwear and knitwear, which
together make up most of the peak season's margin.

It applies to any disruption that threatens product availability during peak: supplier delays,
factory capacity loss, port or canal closures, carrier strikes and sudden demand spikes.

## 2. Principles

1. **Protect the customer promise first.** Stores in A-grade clusters and online orders with a
   promise date are protected before stock is built for B/C-grade clusters.
2. **Use the cheapest lever that closes the gap.** Work through the lever ladder in section 4 in
   order. Only move to a more expensive lever when the cheaper ones cannot close the gap in time.
3. **Decide on evidence, not on noise.** A supplier message or a news item is a signal, not a fact.
   Confirm the delay against the purchase order and the factory schedule before committing spend.
4. **Humans approve spend.** Expedited freight, near-shore buys and production resequencing always
   need approval from the Head of Supply (or a deputy on the peak rota).

## 3. Triage within the first four hours

When a disruption is reported:

- Identify the affected purchase order lines, styles and receiving DCs.
- Estimate the delay in days and how sure we are about it (stated by the supplier, observed by the
  carrier, or assumed).
- Compute the exposure: units on delayed receipts, the first week a DC or cluster runs short, and
  the margin at risk.
- Classify the case: **Watch** (no shortage before W52), **Act** (shortage in peak weeks), or
  **Escalate** (shortage in W47 to W48, or more than 10% of a style's peak demand at risk).

## 4. Lever ladder

| Order | Lever | Typical lead time | Cost profile |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Rebalance stock between DCs (see the DC-to-DC transfer SOP) | 2 to 4 days | Low: road freight per unit |
| 2 | Re-allocate store allocations away from low-grade clusters | 1 to 2 days | Very low: no freight |
| 3 | Targeted sea-air for the most at-risk sizes (see the freight & expedite policy) | 8 to 12 days | Medium to high: premium capped by policy |
| 4 | Near-shore top-up buy from an approved supplier (see the responsible sourcing code) | 3 to 5 weeks | High: unit-cost premium plus MOQ |
| 5 | Resequence production at the factory | Depends on the factory plan | Low cash, but delays other orders |

Combining levers is normal. A typical answer to a one-week trim delay is "rebalance plus targeted
sea-air"; a typical answer to a multi-week factory loss is "near-shore top-up plus resequence".

## 5. Peak freeze rules

- From W46 onwards, no store allocation may be reduced below the cluster's presentation minimum.
- From W47 onwards, transfers into a DC that is itself below its week-cover target are not allowed.
- During W48, only the Head of Supply may approve changes that touch more than 5,000 units.

## 6. Communication

The case owner posts a short status in the peak channel at least daily while a case is open:
what happened, what is at risk, what we decided and what we are waiting for. Customer-facing teams
are told only about changes to online promise dates.

## 7. After the peak

Every case that reached **Act** or **Escalate** gets a short review in January: was the delay
estimate right, which lever worked, and what should change in this playbook.
