## 1. Our code

Every factory that makes product for Demo Retail must meet this code, whether it supplies the
regular range or a one-off top-up during a disruption.

1. **No forced, bonded or child labour.** Workers are at least 18 years old on night shifts and at
   least 16 otherwise, and keep their identity documents.
2. **Fair wages and hours.** At least the legal minimum wage, paid on time; overtime is voluntary,
   paid at a premium and never more than 12 hours a week.
3. **Safe buildings.** A valid structural, fire and electrical safety assessment no older than 24
   months.
4. **Freedom of association** and a working grievance channel.
5. **Environmental basics.** Wastewater treated to the local legal standard; chemicals managed
   against our restricted substances list.
6. **Transparency.** No unauthorised subcontracting. Every production site must be disclosed and
   audited.

## 2. Approval status

A factory may only receive a purchase order when its status is **Approved**. Statuses are:

- **Approved**: a passing audit within the last 12 months and no open critical findings.
- **Conditional**: open major findings with an agreed remediation plan; orders need the Head of
  Sourcing's written sign-off.
- **Blocked**: critical findings or no recent audit. No orders, including during a disruption.

Emergency top-up buys never bypass the approval status. If no approved factory can make the
product in time, the top-up does not happen.

## 4. Using a near-shore top-up

1. Confirm the gap cannot be closed by rebalancing or sea-air within the policy caps.
2. Check the factory's status is still **Approved** on the day of ordering.
3. Order at least the MOQ per style-colour; the top-up quantity reduces the delayed Asian order by
   the same quantity where the supply agreement allows it.
4. The Head of Supply approves the order; the sourcing desk issues the purchase order.

## 5. Breaches

Any breach of this code found during a disruption order is reported to the Responsible Sourcing
team within 24 hours. A disruption is never a reason to accept a breach.
